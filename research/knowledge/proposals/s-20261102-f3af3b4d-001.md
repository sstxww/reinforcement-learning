# s-20261102-f3af3b4d-001 — Treat rules knowledge as versioned, testable data

**Integration proposal only; not verified until engine-backed Actions succeeds.**

The 245 legal actions have distinct source-defined semantic partitions. A masked policy can be legal without understanding the difference between choosing a hero, buying, playing, attaching, moving or ending a turn.

A pinned-engine harness should check (a) source-consistent action names and exact index partitions, (b) `cid+1` embeddings and static catalog provenance, and (c) controlled economic operation invariants against the actual TavernGym rules. These are **mechanism correctness** tests, not policy quality measures.

Use `catalog_json` for card ID/name/tier/type/stats and `implemented`/`scripted` indicators; do not parse flavor/text into guaranteed trigger effects. Effect-specific verified labels require independent transition tests and a source linkage, and multiple variants of the same card may not behave identically. A separate held-out imitation benchmark and real eight-seat results remain essential before promotion.

This proposal avoids overlap with ongoing BC, masked-PPO and policy-action ablations. No claim is made that the generated data improves win rate, models were trained, or TavernArena ranked games occurred.

## Evidence added after completion

[Successful Actions 41422205701](https://github.com/sstxww/reinforcement-learning/actions/runs/41422205701) ran 19 Python tests, pinned engine audit and a genuine full eight-seat game. The catalog has 3983 actual entries, while `N_CARDS=3984` includes padding; the first run failed by misreading that distinction and was repaired from authoritative binding source. In seeded controlled probes, legal masks responded to positive-price affordability, and available Freeze/Upgrade/Buy/Refresh/EndTurn transitions succeeded. The only full-match baseline rank was 8. These findings validate an executable rules foundation, **not** improved gameplay or effect-level correctness.
