# Proposal s-20261102-8cfa0c44-002 — Persistent pool amortizes spawn, not simulation time

**Status:** unmerged research proposal, no competitive strength claim.

## What we actually tested

A two-worker Python ProcessPoolExecutor was reused across four 16-seed batches of **real TavernGym** eight-seat games, compared against four fresh pools and normal serial execution. Two 64-seed trials used opposite order. Seat 0 always used the original baseline; seven opponents were TavernGym scripted bots.

The dedicated, successful [GitHub Actions run 41417665959](https://github.com/sstxww/reinforcement-learning/actions/runs/41417665959) passed 26 tests and completed **385** eight-seat games (one independent + 384 arm games). Engine version 1.1.0 and source hash 6d8712c93022ba11 were verified. All 128 same-seed triplets matched on recorded gameplay/protective fields. Own independent receipt seed 28800: rank 7, 57 decisions, 16 turns, no truncation.

## Numeric findings

Across two trials, 128 games for each method:
- Serial: 0.528338156s.
- Fresh pool every 16 games: 1.781220916s.
- One persistent pool per 64-game trial: 0.663245283s.

Reusing workers made processes about 2.69x faster than repeatedly spawning, but still roughly 1.26x slower in elapsed time than just running the native short games serially. Trial 1 serial/fresh/persistent times: 0.274499/0.908908/0.326013s; trial 2 (reverse order): 0.253839/0.872313/0.337233s.

Identical baseline rank distributions (not three independent policy trials): average seat-0 rank 7.171875, first-place 1/128, top-four 4/128. No PPO steps and no new trained model/checkpoint; no policy improvement claimed. These timings are specific to two CPU trial orders including worker initialization.

## Guidance

For the current extremely fast native TavernGym/V0 baseline, **retain the serial evaluator by default**. A short-batch ProcessPoolExecutor can waste more time starting workers than executing games. Reuse reduces that overhead but was still net negative for 64-game trials on this runner. Do not generalize to large PPO models or longer simulated rollout work without a separate controlled measurement.

Preserve this negative evidence rather than quietly deleting failed speed hypotheses.
