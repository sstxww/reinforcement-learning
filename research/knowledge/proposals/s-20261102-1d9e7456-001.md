# Evidence-backed proposal: purchase-slot selection bias (s-20261102-1d9e7456 R001)

Status: real TavernGym experiment verified and recorded, but claim of competitive performance improvement remains UNVERIFIED. Proposed finding only; integrator decides whether to promote.

## Tested hypothesis
The deterministic baseline always takes first legal purchase slot (indices 8–15), perhaps inducing slot-position bias. Compare baseline with seeded uniform and rightmost buy-slot tie-breaking while leaving all other actions unchanged.

Calibration: 2 real eight-seat training games per baseline, uniform and rightmost. Six games / 355 controlled decisions, zero PPO gradient timesteps. Baseline seed ranks [8,6], uniform [7,7], rightmost [8,5]; selected rightmost based on lowest calibration mean rank (6.5).

## Held-out evidence
Three new paired seeds [38200,38201,38202]:
- Baseline: [8,8,8], mean 8.0, top-four 0/3.
- Rightmost: [5,7,5], mean 5.67, top-four 0/3.
- Observed mean placement difference -2.33 ranks, n=3. This is a limited signal, not a reliable win-rate improvement. Uniform did not undergo independent heldout tests.
- Actual rightmost intervention: 45 tie-breaking opportunities in three held-out games. Baseline counter is instrumented as zero by design, so do NOT interpret it as zero baseline buy actions.
- No game truncations or illegal moves. One complete personally generated match receipt: experiments/s-20261102-1d9e7456/001-38200.json; eight ranks [5,3,8,1,6,7,4,2], seed 38200, 54 decisions.
